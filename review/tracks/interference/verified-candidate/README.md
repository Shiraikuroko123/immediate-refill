# Collecting a prescribed number of verified candidates

Fix an indexed list of \(N\) jobs and a target \(1\le r\le N\). Job \(i\) requires positive work \(w_i\), occupies a slot from admission until completion, and reveals its binary acceptance result only when it finishes. A serial service stops upon the \(r\)-th acceptance or upon processing all \(N\) jobs. Denote this prefix length by \(K\). The parallel service launches in index order and promises to start at most \(K\) jobs on every assignment compatible with its observations. It returns upon obtaining \(r\) acceptances or completing all \(N\) jobs. Both possible outcomes remain feasible for every unfinished job. Each active job advances by the same amount of work in each unit of virtual time.

**Exact admission rule.** Let \(S\) be the completed observations on a reachable history, \(s\) its number of acceptances, \(f\) its number of failures, and \(J\) the cumulative launches. Universal safety of all prior launches implies \(J\le K\) for every completion of the unknown outcomes. Set every unknown outcome to acceptance to minimize \(K\). In this assignment the stopping prefix contains all \(J\) launched indices, and thus all \(f\) observed failures. At the \(r\)-th acceptance the prefix has length \(r+f\), with exhaustion at \(N\) if fewer than \(r\) successes exist. Hence the exact maximum cumulative admissible launch count and the active width after greedy refill are
\[
 F(S)=\min\{N,\,|S|+P,\,r+f\},\qquad
 h_*(S)=F(S)-|S|=\min\{N-|S|,P,r-s\}.
\]
Every launch through \(F\) is universally safe. A launch above it violates the inventory, slot capacity or the serial budget on the all-unknown-accepted completion. Reachability matters here. Observing a failure at a late index before the universal budget permits its launch would invalidate the prefix argument.

**A common prefix.** At all times \(J-|S|\le r-s\). As soon as the service obtains \(r\) acceptances, all admitted jobs have completed. It has processed the entire launched prefix, so the realized \(r\)-th acceptance occurs by \(J\), and the start budget gives \(J\le K\). Thus \(J=K\). On exhaustion it has finished the \(N=K\) jobs unless the target had already been met. All complete schedules run exactly jobs \(1,\ldots,K\) to completion and use the same total work.

**Comparison at every width.** Adding an observation never decreases \(F\). Each term in \(h_*\) stays constant or falls. Greedy on componentwise shorter job work therefore starts every required job no later in virtual time than any feasible schedule on the original work. Indeed, at a comparator launch, each job that has supplied a supporting observation has an earlier launch index and hence, inductively, has already started and finished on shortened greedy. The larger observation set permits at least the same cumulative launch. This also proves that shortened greedy contains all original greedy completions at any fixed virtual time. Its active width can only fall there. Both widths extend by zero after return.

These properties yield the occupancy comparison by the time-compression argument. For any union \(E\) of intervals of a comparator's timeline \([0,D]\), multiply durations outside \(E\) by \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\). Keep every launch and completion at its transformed time. Set each completed job's work to its transformed active duration. Observation order, ties and feasibility persist. On the shortened panel, greedy finishes by transformed time \(D_\epsilon\) with the same work as the transformed comparator and with pointwise width at most the original greedy width. Hence the transformed total work is at most \(\int_0^{D_\epsilon}h_G\). Taking \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) gives \(\int_E h_A\le\int_0^{|E|}h_G\). The threshold decomposition of a discrete concave price makes greedy minimize wall time for every such clock. For a positive clock \(c\) with \(c_0=0\), write \(\widehat c\) for its least concave majorant and \(\gamma=\max_{1\le k\le P}\widehat c_k/c_k\). Then \(T_G(c)\le T_G(\widehat c)\le T_{\rm opt}(\widehat c)\le\gamma T_{\rm opt}(c)\). [Occupancy and threshold argument](../width-generalization/README.md)

**Exact attainment on integer workloads.** For \(\gamma>1\), choose a maximizing \(j\) and a chord \([a,b]\) of the majorant containing \(a<j<b\), with \(\widehat c_a=c_a\) and \(\widehat c_b=c_b\). Let \(m=\operatorname{lcm}(b-a,j-a)\), \(L=m/(j-a)\) and \(D_b=m/(b-a)\). Set \(r=b\) and \(N=a+m\ge b\). Supply \(a\) jobs of work \(L\), followed by \(m\) unit jobs, and make every verification fail. The available active width is always \(\min(P,N-|S|,b)\). Greedy fills \(b\) slots, and its short jobs finish after \(D_b\) virtual units. The long ones finish at \(L\) when \(a>0\). With \(a=0\), extending the profile by zero gives the same cost formula. A comparator caps itself at \(j\) slots and remains at width \(j\) until all jobs finish together at \(L\). The two costs are
\[
 T_G=D_bc_b+(L-D_b)c_a=L\widehat c_j,\qquad T_j=Lc_j.
\]
They give \(T_G/T_j=\gamma\), forcing \(T_j=T_{\rm opt}\) by the proven upper bound. If \(\gamma=1\), every panel already gives the ratio one. Across the class of \(N,r\) on a fixed \(P\)-slot server, \(\gamma\) is attained on a finite instance and greedy is universally optimal exactly for discretely concave clocks. Restricting \(r\) can produce a smaller factor. For example \(r=1\) requires serial execution for every clock.

With \(P=r=N=3\), works \((2,1,1)\), all failures and prices \((10,11,13)\), greedy costs \(13+10=23\) and starting two jobs costs \(2\times11=22\). As an example of the gate itself, take \(r=2\) among \(N=5\) candidates and let the outcomes begin with failure, acceptance, failure, acceptance. If candidate 2 returns first, the remaining acceptance allowance is one, so the scheduler waits for candidate 1. Its failure permits candidate 3. The failure of candidate 3 permits candidate 4, whose acceptance completes the target. The service processes exactly the first four candidates on these outcomes.

The independent finite oracle enumerates compatible full binary assignments at every reached state and compares greedy to all integer-time launch choices with positive active width. It verifies the exact frontier, terminal prefix and optimality of each convex occupancy threshold feature, with 4,740 panel configurations and 6,752 threshold comparisons. A second calculation checks the integer construction for all 210 endpoint triples across \(2\le P\le8\), including clocks with ratio exactly two. The continuous-time statement follows from the proof above. An actual generation or verification service would additionally need known job order, terminal acceptance, and the per-question launch promise for this interpretation to apply.
