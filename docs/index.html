LLM inference / Scheduling theory

When is immediate refill optimal for parallel voting?

Launching one more reasoning path can slow every path already running. We characterize when filling a free slot is fastest, and test the prediction on an NVIDIA A800.

The optimality condition

The shape of the decoding cost curve

Suppose requests start in a fixed order and no extra launches are allowed beyond the serial voting budget. For a fixed panel size, slot count and launch quota, immediate refill is fastest on every input exactly when the step costs are discretely concave over the reachable widths.

What if the curve is not concave?

Keep the slot count and the step costs fixed, and allow panel size and launch quota to vary. The largest ratio of the least concave majorant to the actual cost gives the exact worst-case latency ratio for immediate refill.

Discrete concavity

2ck ≥ ck−1 + ck+1

Here cₖ is the time for one decoding step with k active requests. At costs 10, 11 and 13, a third request adds more step time than the second.

Delay the third request

For three requests of lengths 256, 128 and 128, the calibrated clock predicted a 3.13% saving. In a fresh engine process on the same A800, all 12 paired blocks favored the delayed launch.

Use votes as they arrive

Across 200 GSM8K problems, using all returned votes saved 8.71% relative to waiting for a contiguous prefix. The median was 0.14%. Once a comparator kept the prefix and counted the other returned votes, full indexing saved a further 0.30% relative to it.

How much can better timing recover?

With vote information held fixed on 200 archived 16-path panels, the work needed to certify a winner bounds the mean saving. An oracle choosing one wait per panel reached 0.1145%, bracketing the offline optimum under this clock.

200 problems · one extra launch allowed · offline optimum

Limits of the measurements

In a separate fixed-workload A800 comparison of admission policies on 16 problems, the mean saving was 0.55% (95% interval −0.03% to 1.29%). For 200 archived three-request panels, a length-aware choice between immediate launch and the first completion saved 0.364% on average. Each comparison uses its own baseline.
